01Thesisgoal & context · scope: business

Every login shows the degraded dot for about a minute with nothing wrong. The listener pushes its sender keys one second before omarchy-shell can answer, records the hold as unavailable, and waits a full 60-second period to retry. The fix shortens that minute to seconds and reserves the "run setup" notice for a shell that really lacks the feature.

One load-bearing distinction. The hold module today collapses every push failure into one unavailable result. The fix separates a transport failure (shell unreachable, timed out, call failed) from an answered failure (shell replied with an error, a missing command, or an unparseable payload). Only transport failures earn the short retry. The state file, status output and bar widget from fn-26 do not change.

04Edge cases & constraints4 items
new episode
A mid-session shell restart is a new episode with its own window.
coupling
A short retry re-runs only the shell push, never the sound-mute sync, which scans the audio server.
accepted bound
Each shell call blocks the listener for up to the IPC timeout, longer than the status ping allows; a hanging shell can make the listener look unresponsive for that long. Documented, not fixed here.
startup answers
An "error" or empty answer during startup counts as answered; a wrong guess costs the per-period retry, which is today's behaviour.
05Boundariesscope: business
this spec is
  • A bounded 2 s / 10 s retry after a transport failure
  • An internal transport-vs-answered classification in the hold module
  • The notice reserved for answered failures
  • New fake-shell modes and the tests in R4
this spec is not
  • A change to fn-26's health model, state-file fields, status output, or bar widget; unavailable still means degraded
  • A change to the 60-second period for the other services
  • A non-blocking rewrite of the shell call
06Decision contextflat · reviewed before write
D1Bounded retry, per-period push stays the steady state.
A shell that is truly gone costs nothing beyond the window.
Rejected: waiting for the shell before the first push (the listener cannot know when, and a shell restart repeats the race).
D2Keep unavailable as the recorded state during the window.
Rejected: reporting the transient as unknown, a new state for a condition that lasts seconds.
D32 seconds and 10 seconds.
Two seconds fits the one-second tick with half the subprocess churn; ten seconds gives five attempts before the 60-second schedule resumes.
